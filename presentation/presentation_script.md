## Slide 5: Pipeline Overview
**Visuals:** Pipeline diagram (D2/Mermaid visual).  
**Duration:** 1:45  

**Script:**  
Here is an overview of our pipeline. The workflow is designed across four functional blocks:
1. **Preprocessing:** We perform resizing and Z-score normalization to prepare the data for the CNN backbones.
2. **Feature Extraction:** This is the most critical part. We employ ResNet50 and InceptionV3 as **Semantic Encoders**. Instead of using the final classification layer, we extract the 2048-dimensional feature vector immediately following the Global Average Pooling layer.
3. **Geometric Analysis:** We contrast PCA (maximizing global variance) with UMAP (preserving local neighborhood structures).
4. **Dual-Track ML:** We execute parallel classification on the original 2048-D space and a compressed 3D space to evaluate the trade-off between information density and performance.

## Slide 6: 3D Manifold Visualizations (UMAP)
**Visuals:** ResNet50 vs InceptionV3 3D plots.  
**Duration:** 1:30  

**Script:**  
Looking at the 3D visualizations generated by the UMAP algorithm, you can see that the deep features do not distribute randomly. They converge into distinct, well-defined clusters according to their class labels.

Although traditional metrics like the Silhouette Score might yield negative values in pure Euclidean space (due to complex non-linear structures), topologically, the classes are well-separated. This proves that pre-trained CNNs extract highly valuable semantic information, which serves as the foundation for applying non-linear SVM kernels.

## Slide 7: Classification in 3D Space
**Visuals:** Table with accuracy (SGDC, SVM-RBF, Random Forest).  
**Duration:** 1:45  

**Script:**  
An interesting finding during our experiments was the performance in 3-dimensional space. Using linear models like SGD, accuracy was below 35%, indicating that the data is significantly "folded" or non-linear in 3D.

However, when using **Random Forest**, the accuracy soared to **82.28%** on the ResNet50 architecture. Why is this? It's because Random Forest uses axis-aligned partitioning, which is exceptionally well-suited for "encircling" the high-density clusters created by UMAP. This result demonstrates that with just 3 dimensions (a mere 0.14% of the original volume), we can still retain most of the classification power.

## Slide 8: High-D Classification Results
**Visuals:** Results table (InceptionV3 + RBF: 97.24%), Confusion Matrix.  
**Duration:** 1:45  

**Script:**  
Moving to the results in the full 2048-dimensional space. To ensure statistical reliability, these metrics were evaluated on a \textbf{stratified 20\% test set} and further verified through \textbf{5-fold Cross-Validation}.

As expected, non-linear models dominate. The combination of \textbf{InceptionV3 and the RBF Kernel} achieved a peak accuracy of \textbf{97.24\%}. Using the RBF kernel allows us to find more complex decision boundaries in high-dimensional space, effectively resolving errors between visually similar signs like 30 km/h and 50 km/h—which are typical weak points for standard linear classifiers.
